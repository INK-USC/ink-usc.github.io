Semester Course Project

The project is 55% of the course grade. Students will work in teams of six to design and carry out a research or application project that answers a question in natural language processing.

Project Types

  • Research-focused: Develop models and analyze data for an existing NLP problem, or formulate a new problem.
  • Application-focused: Train or fine-tune and deploy NLP models in a new application area using tools and techniques from the course.

Project Proposal

State whether the project is research- or application-focused, then clearly address: the objective in jargon-free language; current practice and its limits; what is new and why it may succeed; who cares and what success would change; risks and early pivots; time and computational resources; feasibility within one semester; and two milestones.

Midterm Update Presentation

One team member may present for the team. The presentation is graded on clarity, novelty, and presentation quality.

  • Slide 1: The problem, method, and differentiation from prior work.
  • Slide 2: The main takeaway so far and what remains before the final report.

Midterm Report

Explain the objective and significance in detail; cover related work; describe the proposed method and its novelty; specify experiments, hypotheses, and evaluation; report preliminary results when available; revisit proposal milestones; document progress; and provide updated milestones for the rest of the semester.

Final Presentation

Two or three team members may present. The presentation is graded on clarity, completeness, and presentation quality. It should convey the main takeaway, motivation and background, the key insight beyond prior work, technical details, and any work remaining before the report.

Final Report

The final report should revise the midterm report in response to feedback and explain: the objective and significance; limits of current practice; the new approach and why it was expected to work; experiments, hypotheses, evaluation, and results; discoveries and support for the hypotheses; the main takeaways; next steps; and new research questions raised by the work.
